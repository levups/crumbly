Benchmarks

Crumbly against gretel, on the same trails.

Rendering a trail

bench/render.rb renders the same five-level trail (Home > Customers > Alice > Orders > Order 2026-0042) with gretel and with Crumbly, and checks that both give the same output. The <ol> row compares gretel's breadcrumbs(style: :ol) with a ten-line helper over breadcrumb_trail. The JSON-LD row compares gretel's structured_data(url_base:), with paths behind a prefix, and breadcrumb_json_ld with _url helpers. Both are encoded and escaped the same way.

Ruby 4.0.9, Rails 8.1.4, gretel 5.1.0, Apple M5 (arm64 macOS), median of three runs of benchmark-ips
RenderAllocationsTime, YJIT offTime, YJIT on
gretel <ol>34557.6 µs35.1 µs
Crumbly <ol>27445.4 µs29.8 µs
gretel JSON-LD26841.3 µs25.4 µs
Crumbly JSON-LD28743.3 µs29.5 µs

Crumbly's JSON-LD is a little slower, mostly because Rails' _url helpers are slower than _path ones. Building the BreadcrumbList from the levels takes 1.7 µs of the difference.

Running it again

From a checkout of the gem, once with YJIT off and once with it on:

bundle exec ruby bench/render.rb
bundle exec ruby --yjit bench/render.rb

Run each three times and keep the median. The numbers depend on the machine, so compare the two gems on the same one rather than with this table.
